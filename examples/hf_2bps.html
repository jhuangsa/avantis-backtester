    How a rule gets in and out

    All five trade the ETH perpetual. A basis point is 0.01%, so 10 bps is a tenth of a percent. The fee is 1 bp when the trade opens and 1 bp when it closes.

    The decision waits for a bar to finish. The fill is the open of the next bar. If a bar touches both the profit target and the stop, the stop is the exit. Whichever exit comes first closes the whole trade.

    Sharpe in the tables is annualized from the closed trades. It is the mean trade divided by the standard deviation of the trades, multiplied by the square root of how many of those trades fit in a year, counted from the first entry to the last.

    1

    New York session, long ETH

    15-minute bars, long only, target 0.8%, stop 0.3%, flat at 20:00 UTC.

    Confirmed on the later fifth. Invalidated on the whole book.

    The rule

    1. The idea. Buy ETH once every afternoon, at the start of New York hours, and get out the same day. There is no chart pattern. The clock is the signal.
    2. When. Every UTC day, including weekends. The session is 13:30 to 20:00 UTC, about 9:30am to 4:00pm in New York.
    3. Get in. Find the first 15-minute bar that opens at or after 13:30. When that bar closes, buy at the open of the next bar, usually 13:45. One buy a day. If that next open is already 20:00 or later, skip the day.
    4. Get out. Sell at the first of these: price is 0.8% above the entry, price is 0.3% below the entry, or the clock reaches 20:00. A 26-bar limit, 6.5 hours, is also set. On a full day, 20:00 comes first. In this test that limit never closed a trade.
    5. The position. Long only. A second signal the same day is ignored.

    Across 631 days the average trade loses 0.73 bps. From 20 May 2026, 126 trades average +10.03 bps. 2025 alone is −3.58 bps. Of the 631 trades, 442 hit the 0.3% stop, 170 hit the 0.8% target, and 19 were sold at 20:00. The dashed line is the same buy with the stop tightened to 0.2%.

    2

    Donchian breakout, long ETH

    15-minute bars, long only, 116-bar channel, target 6 ATR, stop 2 ATR, 196 bars.

    Weak. The out-of-sample mean is large and the t-statistic is 1.88.

    The rule

    1. The idea. Buy ETH when it breaks above the highest high of roughly the last day, and give the trade room to run.
    2. The ceiling. On every 15-minute bar, all day, the ceiling is the highest high of the previous 116 bars, about 29 hours. The bar you are judging is left out of that high.
    3. Get in. When the close crosses up through that ceiling, buy at the next bar’s open. The position is long only.
    4. How far. The target and the stop scale with recent noise. ATR is the average true range of the last 14 bars, the typical distance from a bar’s high to its low. The target sits 6 ATRs above the entry. The stop sits 2 ATRs below it.
    5. Get out. Sell at the target, at the stop, or after 196 bars, about 49 hours, whichever comes first. A break of the channel low does not close the trade.

    83 out-of-sample trades average +33.98 bps. Confirmed asks for a t of 2, and this one is 1.88. The whole book is 388 trades, +10.70 bps, t = 1.18. Four trades lasted the full 196 bars. The other 384 hit the target or the stop: 109 targets, 275 stops.

    3

    RSI reclaim, gated by the average

    5-minute bars, both sides, RSI 7, EMA 21, target 1.25%, stop 0.30%, 26 bars.

    Weak on the whole book. The out-of-sample slice has 76 trades, short of 80, and t is 0.23.

    The rule

    1. The idea. Buy a dip that is turning up inside an uptrend. Short a spike that is turning down inside a downtrend. Both sides trade. The bar size is 5 minutes.
    2. RSI(7). A score from 0 to 100 of whether the last few closes were mostly rising or mostly falling. Under 30 means a recent slide. Over 70 means a recent run-up.
    3. The average. EMA(21) is a smoothed line of the last 21 closes. It is only a filter for direction. Above it, the rule will buy. Below it, the rule will short.
    4. Get long. RSI crosses up through 30, and the close is above the average. Buy the next open.
    5. Get short. RSI crosses down through 70, and the close is below the average. Short the next open.
    6. Get out. Up 1.25%, down 0.30%, or 26 bars (130 minutes), whichever comes first. A signal the other way leaves the open trade alone.

    The later slice has 76 trades, short of the 80 required before an out-of-sample result can be Confirmed. The score therefore uses all 352 trades: +5.01 bps, t = 1.66. Out of sample the mean is +1.33 bps and t is 0.23. Stops took 212 trades, targets 51, and the 130-minute clock 89.

    4

    BTC minus ETH, faded on the ETH leg

    5-minute bars, both sides, z window 48, entry at 1.5, target 0.8%, stop 0.3%, 24 bars.

    Invalidated. A positive out-of-sample mean, and a t-statistic of 0.94.

    The rule

    1. The idea. When Bitcoin gets unusually expensive relative to ETH, buy ETH. When ETH gets unusually expensive relative to Bitcoin, short ETH. The bet is that the gap between them eases.
    2. What is traded. ETH only. Bitcoin is read and never bought or sold. Profit and loss are the ETH position alone.
    3. The gap. Every 5 minutes, take the natural log of the BTC close and subtract the natural log of the ETH close. Compare that number with the last 48 bars, which is 4 hours. The z-score says how many standard deviations the latest gap sits from that recent average. The standard deviation divides by 48.
    4. Get long ETH. The z-score crosses up through +1.5. ETH is the cheap leg, so you buy it at the next open.
    5. Get short ETH. The z-score crosses down through −1.5. ETH is the rich leg, so you short it.
    6. Get out. Up 0.8%, down 0.3%, or 24 bars (2 hours), whichever comes first.

    1,357 out-of-sample trades average +1.10 bps, and t is 0.94. The whole join is 7,559 trades at −0.61 bps. 2025 loses. 2026 is flat. The later mean is positive, and the test still fails.

    5

    Weekend gap, faded

    1-minute bars, once a weekend, gap of 60 bps, target 0.5%, stop 0.3%, three hours.

    Invalidated. Sixty-nine weekends, t = 0.82. The later twelve are inconclusive.

    The rule

    1. The idea. If ETH moved a lot from Friday’s close to Monday’s open, bet that part of the move comes back.
    2. The weekend move. Friday’s last 1-minute close, against Monday’s first 1-minute open, both in UTC. Saturday and Sunday prices are skipped, so the number is the change across the whole weekend. The tape itself does not stop.
    3. Get in. Trade only if that move is at least 60 bps, which is 0.60%. A drop of that size is a buy. A rise of that size is a short. The fill is the open of the next minute. One trade each weekend.
    4. Get out. Up 0.5%, down 0.3%, or 180 minutes, whichever comes first.

    69 weekends qualify. The average is +3.94 bps. Twelve fall after the cut, which is too few to score. Every one of the 69 hit the target or the stop inside three hours: 31 targets and 38 stops.

    6

    Lighter Donchian

    Four daily breakouts vote. Equal risk. Premium fee, half the spread, and funding.

    Verdict on the closed trades: Invalidated. The book’s own Sharpe over all 56 sessions is −0.01.

    The rule

    1. The idea. Four trend systems each vote on every Lighter perpetual that has enough history. You hold the average of the votes, and you size each name so that a quiet name and a wild name risk the same fraction of equity.
    2. One vote. The 10-bar system goes long when the daily close breaks above the high of the previous 10 daily bars, and short when it breaks the low. It stays in that position until the opposite break. The 20-bar, 50-bar, and 100-bar systems do the same. The vote you trade is the average of the four, so it steps from −1 to +1 by quarters.
    3. When you trade. The break is known at the close. The position starts at the next day’s open and is marked to the open after that.
    4. How big. Weight equals the vote times a risk budget, divided by that name’s 14-day average true range. The budget is set so the book would run near 10% annualized volatility if the names did not move together. They do move together: realized volatility was 18%. No name exceeds 15% of equity. Average gross exposure was 0.80.
    5. What it costs. A premium Lighter account pays a 2.8 bp taker fee on the notional that changes hands. The book also pays half that name’s median bid-ask spread, and Lighter’s hourly funding. A long pays when the funding rate is positive. A standard account pays no fee; spread and funding remain.
    6. The tape. Lighter’s own prices, spreads, and funding start on 30 July 2026. A 100-bar channel is older than that tape, so the earlier highs and lows come from the matching Avantis daily series, rescaled into Lighter’s price. 80 names kept a stable scale: 51 crypto, 23 equities, 4 FX, 2 other. Each row in the table is one name, held one way, until that vote flips or goes flat.

    How a cell was judged

    Fewer than 30 trades is inconclusive. Confirmed needs a positive mean, a t-statistic of at least 2, and every year that has 10 or more trades to be positive itself. With four or more such years, three positive years are enough. The t-statistic divides by the sample standard deviation.

    The out-of-sample window is the last 20% of the calendar, from 20 May 2026 at 19:12 UTC. If that window has fewer than 80 trades, the whole book is what gets judged, and a result that would have been Confirmed is called Weak instead.

    Weak, on this page, is a positive mean with t at least 1 and short of Confirmed. That cut sits between the Donchian whole-book t of 1.18 and the residual out-of-sample t of 0.94, which is how those two published labels separate.

    A 5-minute or 15-minute bar is kept only when its closing minute is present. Volume in these candles is zero, so none of the five cells uses it. Fills are the next bar’s open. A stop or a target that the bar’s range touches is filled at the level, and a stop wins if both are touched. A trade still open at the last bar is marked at the close and pays no exit fee.
